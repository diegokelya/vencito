IN THE PANTRY

How to organize the pantry so nothing expires unused

What expires first goes in front, new items go in the back, and you check what you already have before you buy more.

November 13, 2026

Using what you already bought also depends on where you put it. Yogurt, sauce, dish soap, or the jar in the medicine cabinet: if the new package hides the old one, the one at the back expires before anyone sees it. Organizing means leaving what you should use first where you can see it.

This note is a guide to sorting what you already have at home. It is not medical or legal advice, and it does not replace what the package says or the rules where you live. If something looks wrong, smells off, or the package is damaged, do not use it. If you are unsure about your health, ask someone qualified.

What expires first goes in front

When you come back from the store, new items go in the back. Move what was already there toward the front, and leave the package you just bought behind it. The next time you open the pantry, the first thing you see is what expires sooner.

The same move works in the fridge and the freezer: yogurt that was already there in front, the one you just brought home at the back. On a deep shelf, what stays hidden is what goes off.

A place for each thing

Grouping by place makes it easier to see what you have, and it keeps food away from what you do not eat.

  • Pantry. Dry goods and sealed packages: rice, cans, pasta, sauces, cookies.
  • Fridge. What the package says to keep cold: dairy, opened jars, cold cuts.
  • Freezer. Frozen food, with the date in sight. An unlabeled package gets lost among the others.
  • Cleaning. Dish soap, bleach, and the rest, away from food. They do not share a shelf with what you eat.
  • Medicine cabinet. Medicines stay there, not with the food.

You do not need a perfect system. It is enough that each group has a spot and that, inside that spot, whatever expires next stays in front.

Check what you already have before you buy

The extra jar is the one that later expires unopened. Before you go out, open the pantry and the fridge and see what is still full. If you already have two jars of tomato sauce, the third can wait.

A short list is enough: write down what you are actually out of, not what you already have just in case.

A quick look once a week

Once a week, go through the pantry, the fridge, and the freezer and look for what expires soon. It is not a full inventory. Noticing three or four things and using them in the next few days is enough.

Once you can read the date on the package, the next step is to see what expires first. How to read it is in How to read the expiration date.

If the date has already passed, do not reconstruct it from memory: follow what the package says. And if it looks wrong, smells off, or the package is damaged, do not use it.
